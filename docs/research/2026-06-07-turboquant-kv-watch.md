## What it is (verified)
TurboQuant (Zandieh, Daliri, Hadian, Mirrokni — Google Research / DeepMind, Daliri at NYU; **arXiv:2504.19874**, submitted 2025-04-28; *"to be presented at ICLR 2026"* per the Google Research blog dated 2026-03-24) is a **data-free, online vector-quantization** method. It randomly rotates each KV vector (inducing a concentrated Beta distribution on coordinates), applies a per-coordinate optimal scalar quantizer, then adds a **1-bit QJL** transform on the residual to make inner-product estimation unbiased; it proves near-optimal distortion (within a ≈2.7 constant of the information-theoretic bound). For KV-cache the **paper claims, verbatim: "absolute quality neutrality with 3.5 bits per channel and marginal quality degradation with 2.5 bits per channel"** — no fine-tuning. Siblings: **QJL** (the residual transform) and **PolarQuant** (separate paper, "to be presented at AISTATS 2026").

## Why it matters HERE
Memory is the binding constraint on a single 128 GB Spark: **E20** records that the 35B resident and the 120B on-demand each occupy ~65–75 GB with serving budgets, so co-residency only works because the 120B is strictly on-demand with constrained KV. KV cache at **BF16** grows with context and competes for exactly that headroom. A *held-quality* ~3.5-bit KV would be **≈4.6× smaller than BF16** (16 ÷ 3.5; the operator's "~4.5×") — directly buying back co-residency headroom and/or longer context.

More important, it **re-opens the E1 question**. E1 pinned the resident KV to BF16 because **FP8-KV degraded quality** (4/8 failed runs vs 1/8 for BF16, +0.2 t/s = noise). TurboQuant's central claim is *quality neutrality at far lower bit-width* — i.e., it claims to avoid the precise failure mode that sank FP8-KV. **If** that claim holds **on Qwen3.6 and on SM121**, it is a legitimate challenger to the pin. Both conditions are unproven for our setup today.

*(Note on provenance: the operator referenced a "~36 GB / 1M tokens BF16 KV per E30" figure; **E30 is in the reserved E28–E32 range and is not landed in this registry**, so it is treated here as operator context, not a cited registry fact. The in-registry memory basis is E20 + E1.)*

## Watch-item — promotion gates (this is a watch-item, NOT a recommendation)

The BF16-KV pin (E1) does **not** move until all three gates pass, in order:

- **G1 — Mainstream, supported, at the quality-neutral width.** TurboQuant lands in mainstream vLLM (not a fork) as a *supported, stable* KV path **at ≥3.5-bit-equivalent quality-neutral mode**.
  *Status 2026-06-07: PARTIALLY met and insufficient.* 2-bit is merged (#38479) + hybrid support (#39931), but the quality-neutral 3/4-bit grouped modes were **closed un-merged** (#39890), the feature issue is still **open** (#38171), and there is an active bug cluster. The merged 2-bit width is **below** the paper's quality-neutral threshold — it would walk straight into the E1 degradation we pinned against. **G1 = NOT met.**
- **G2 — SM121/GB10 evidence.** Any credible TurboQuant-on-SM121/GB10 (DGX Spark) compatibility signal.
  *Status 2026-06-07: **MET at the "credible signal" bar** (corrected — see methodology note).* Concrete SM121/GB10 results exist: the Sggin1 comment on #38479 with a TQ3-vs-FP8 table, the `Sggin1/DGX-SPARK` patch repo, and NVIDIA forum threads #365627 / #364736. **But** the evidence is single-hobbyist, **Triton-fallback (CUDA kernels not compiled for SM121)**, on Nemotron/Qwen3.5/GLM — not our Qwen3.6 resident, throughput/NIAH only, no quality eval. So G2 (as written: "any credible compatibility signal") is satisfied; **G1 (mainstream-supported at the quality-neutral width, with real SM121 kernels) and G3 (golden-set bake-off) are NOT** — those remain what actually gates the pin.
- **G3 — Bake-off, only after G1+G2.** Evaluate via the **ADR-0001 bake-off** pattern. It must pass, on our box: the **Qwen3.6 golden set** (the E1 evidence basis), the **garbage canary including the semantic / golden-set checks** (E37 — a `!!!`/repetition canary cannot catch the quality drift this would risk), and a **long-context needle test**. Only if all three pass is the BF16-KV pin even **re-discussed** — not flipped.

**Cheap probe (post-hardware):** G2's generic "does it run on SM121?" question is already answered (Sggin1 + forum #365627). The refined probe: (1) reproduce **on our Qwen3.6-35B-A3B resident specifically** — their results are Nemotron/Qwen3.5/GLM; and (2) watch for **compiled SM121 CUDA kernels** replacing the current Triton fallback. Both are prerequisites for moving past G2 toward G1/G3.
